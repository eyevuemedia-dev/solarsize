How many solar panels to run a laptop?

Very few. A 13-inch MacBook Air uses about 23–59 Wh a day in normal use, which is 6–16 W of panels at the U.S. average. Gaming laptops are the exception: two hours at a 330 W adapter limit is about 660 Wh, or about 177 W of panels. Whatever you run, charge over USB-C when you can, because an inverter left on can use more power than the laptop.

Short answer

  • Thin-and-light laptop: Apple measures the 13-inch MacBook Air (M5) at 3.69 W from the wall with the screen on, and ENERGY STAR rates its typical use at 8.3 kWh a year (about 23 Wh a day). Even draining and refilling its 53.8 Wh battery every day is only about 59 Wh. A single 50–100 W panel covers it most of the year.
  • Why the old number was 360 Wh: it assumed the 60 W adapter maximum for 6 hours. That is a ceiling for fast charging, not what the laptop draws while you type. We now show it as the worst case.
  • Gaming laptop: MSI lists a 330 W adapter and up to 250 W of combined CPU and GPU power for its RTX 5080 Raider 16 HX. Plan on 250–330 W while gaming, and size the station's AC output above 330 W.
  • USB-C beats AC: most power stations burn 15–40 W just keeping the inverter on. For a 4 W laptop, that idle is 4–10 times the laptop's own draw. A 100 W USB-C port skips it.
  • Our pick: for work trips and outages, a 256–512 Wh station with a 100 W USB-C port and a 100 W panel. For gaming, a 1 kWh-class station with 1,500 W or more continuous and 200–400 W of panels.

How much energy a laptop uses

A laptop's adapter rating tells you the most it can deliver, usually while fast-charging a flat battery with the processor busy. Day to day, a modern thin-and-light machine draws a small fraction of that. Apple ships the 13-inch MacBook Air (M5) with a "40W Dynamic Power Adapter with 60W Max" and a 53.8 Wh battery rated for up to 15 hours of wireless web (Apple specs); 53.8 Wh over 15 hours is about 3.6 W from the battery. Apple's environmental report measures the same machine at 3.69 W from the wall with the display on, 0.22 W asleep and 0.10 W off, and the adapter at 91.3% efficient (Apple).

USB-C vs the AC outlet: the inverter idle trap

Plugging a laptop's brick into a power station's AC outlet converts battery DC to 120 V AC and back to 20 V DC. Each step loses a little, but the big cost is the inverter's own draw. Willrun's station data puts AC idle at 15–40 W across popular models (idle table). That is fine for a 1,000 W load and ruinous for a 4 W one.

Worked example on a 1,024 Wh EcoFlow DELTA 2: a MacBook Air at 3.69 W over USB-C runs about 236 hours of light use per charge. Through the AC outlet with a 15 W idle on top, it is about 46 hours, roughly a fifth as long. The same logic applies on a DIY 12 V system: a USB-C PD car charger or a fused 12 V-to-USB-C converter beats running a 1,000 W inverter all day for a laptop.

USB-C has limits. The USB Power Delivery spec now goes up to 240 W (48 V at 5 A) with rated cables (USB-IF), but power-station USB-C ports in our database top out at 100–140 W, and most gaming laptops still use a barrel-plug adapter for full power. Apple says the MacBook Air fast-charges from a 70 W or higher USB PD source, so a 100 W port is plenty.

Gaming laptops on solar

A high-end gaming laptop on full power is a serious load. MSI's Raider 16 HX with an RTX 5080 Laptop GPU lists a 330 W adapter, a 91.8 Wh battery and up to 250 W of combined CPU and GPU power (MSI specs). Two hours of play at 250–330 W is 500–660 Wh: about {panel(500,US):.0f}–{panel(660,US):.0f} W of panels at the U.S. average, or {panel(500,DET):.0f}–{panel(660,DET):.0f} W in a Detroit December.

  • Battery-saver and frame caps help a lot. Capping frame rate or switching to the laptop's balanced or silent profile cuts GPU power, and the energy drops with it. Measure your own setup with a plug-in watt meter to see by how much.
  • Don't game on battery alone. A 91.8 Wh battery at 250 W lasts well under half an hour, and most gaming laptops throttle hard when unplugged.
  • Size the AC output, not just the Wh. Pick a station with continuous AC well above 330 W. Every 1 kWh-class station in the table qualifies; the 300 W class does not.

Can you plug a solar panel straight into a laptop?

Some folding panels have a USB-C output, and on a clear day they can charge a phone or a light laptop. The weak point is clouds: panel output follows the sun minute by minute, so a passing cloud can drop below what the laptop negotiated and charging stops and restarts. A small battery or power station between panel and laptop smooths that out, stores midday surplus for the evening, and lets you use the 100 W port at night. For a gaming laptop, direct panel charging is not practical.

Tips that save the most energy

  • Use USB-C or 12 V DC whenever the laptop supports it, and switch the station's AC off.
  • Turn screen brightness down. On a thin-and-light laptop the display is one of the largest loads at idle.
  • Sleep, don't idle. Apple measures 0.22 W asleep against 3.69 W idle with the screen on.
  • Charge at midday when the panels are producing, so the battery isn't cycled twice.
  • Measure a real week with a USB-C power meter or plug-in watt meter. Your number beats any estimate here.

Questions

How many solar panels to run a laptop?

Usually one small panel. A 13-inch MacBook Air uses about 23–59 Wh a day in normal use, which is about 6–16 W of panels at the U.S. average of 4.98 sun hours. A gaming laptop playing for 2 hours near its 330 W adapter limit needs about 177 W.

Where do the figures come from?

Apple's MacBook Air (M5) environmental report measures 3.69 W from the wall with the display on and a 91.3%-efficient adapter; ENERGY STAR lists its typical energy consumption at 8.3 kWh a year; Apple's spec page lists a 53.8 Wh battery and a 40 W adapter with 60 W max. Gaming figures use MSI's 330 W adapter and 250 W CPU-plus-GPU rating for the Raider 16 HX.

How long will a power station run a laptop?

Over USB-C, roughly capacity × 0.85 ÷ the laptop's draw. A 1,024 Wh station runs a MacBook Air at 3.69 W for about 236 hours of light use, or recharges it about 16 times. Through the AC outlet the inverter's 15–40 W idle cuts that to a fraction.

Should I charge my laptop from the AC outlet or USB-C?

USB-C, or a 12 V USB-C PD charger, whenever the laptop supports it. The AC route adds the inverter's 15–40 W idle draw plus two conversion losses, which can be more than a light laptop uses.

Can a 300 W power station run a gaming laptop?

Not reliably at full load. A gaming laptop with a 330 W adapter can pull more than a 300 W inverter's continuous rating, and the station may shut off mid-game. Choose a station rated well above the adapter's wattage.

Can I charge a laptop directly from a solar panel?

A panel with a USB-C output can charge a light laptop in steady sun, but clouds interrupt charging. A small power station or battery between the panel and the laptop gives steady output and stores energy for evening use.
