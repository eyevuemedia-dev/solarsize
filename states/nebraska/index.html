How much solar do I need in Nebraska?

Short answer: Nebraska averages 4.98 peak sun hours and 1,423 kWh a year per kW, so a home using 10 kWh a day needs about 2,677 W of panels. That sun-hour figure is the U.S. average this site uses for appliance math. It is not a December day: the same array makes about 7.2 kWh on an average December day in Omaha.

Average sun, above-average purchases

The table at the top uses the site formula and is unchanged. Panel watts = daily Wh ÷ (4.98 × 0.75). A 10 kWh day is 10,000 ÷ 3.735 = 2,677 W, about 7 panels of 400 W, and 12.35 kWh of battery for one day with no recharge. 4.98 peak sun hours is also the U.S. average on the appliance pages, so a 55-inch TV at 103 W of panels, or any other appliance sized at 4.98 hours, is already the Nebraska annual case. Do not "adjust for Nebraska sun" on top of those pages.

The average Nebraska residential customer bought 956 kWh a month in 2024, bill $110 (EIA Table 5A). That is 11,472 kWh a year, against a U.S. average of 863 kWh a month. Divided by 1,423 kWh per kW, covering those purchases takes about 8.1 kW, or 21 panels of 400 W. The sun is ordinary. The purchases are not, and the purchases set the system size. Omaha's year, 1,431 kWh per kW, is within 1% of the state figure of 1,423, so the published average reads like eastern Nebraska. Scottsbluff's year is 1,575, about 11% above the state PVWatts figure. That east-west gap is real and much smaller than Oregon's Portland-to-Bend gap. It is not a reason to ignore winter. Omaha in December is 2.70 kWh per kW, and July is 4.58. A 2,677 W array makes about 7.2 kWh on an average December day (2.677 × 2.70) and about 12.3 kWh on an average July day (2.677 × 4.58). Annual grid-tied sizing assumes summer kWh can offset winter kWh on the bill. It does not describe an off-grid December, and it does not describe a week of snow on a low-tilt array. PVGIS does not model snow cover; the cabin guide says the same about its own runs.

Cold is a battery limit, not a panel-watt limit

The 12.35 kWh figure is nameplate energy for one sunless day after a 0.9 depth-of-discharge and 0.9 inverter allowance. It says nothing about charging below freezing. Many LiFePO4 packs block charging near 32°F unless they are heated. Datasheet limits are in the battery chemistry guide. Keep a bank that lives in an unheated shed inside the temperature window the maker prints, or the December solar resource never reaches the cells. Neighboring rows for context, not as substitutes: Kansas (5.39 peak sun hours) and South Dakota (4.86). The formula is on the methodology page.

Limits

  • 956 kWh a month is an average of customers, not your house. Irrigation, a shop, or electric heat can dwarf it. Use 12 months of your own kWh.
  • Snow days fall below the 2.70 kWh December average. The battery and, off-grid, a generator are what cover those stretches.
  • No state incentive or production-credit dollars are included in the wattage.

Questions

How many peak sun hours does Nebraska get?

4.98 kWh per square meter per day, from NREL PVWatts v8, and 1,423 kWh a year per kW. That is the same U.S. average this site uses on appliance pages, so those panel-watt figures already match Nebraska's annual sun.

How many solar panels for a house in Nebraska?

The average Nebraska customer bought 956 kWh a month in 2024 (EIA Table 5A), 11,472 kWh a year, with a $110 average bill. Divided by 1,423 kWh per kW, that is about 8.1 kW, or 21 panels of 400 W. Sun here is near the U.S. average of 4.98 peak sun hours. Use, at 956 kWh a month against a U.S. average of 863, is what makes the system this size.

How short is December compared with July?

At a south-facing latitude tilt, PVGIS puts Omaha (41.26°N, 95.94°W) at 2.70 kWh per kW on an average December day and 4.58 in July. A 2,677 W array sized for 10 kWh a day makes about 7.2 kWh on that December day and about 12.3 kWh on that July day. Scottsbluff (41.87°N, 103.67°W) produces about 1,575 kWh per kW per year, and December there is 2.89 kWh per kW. PVGIS does not subtract snow cover.

Will the battery charge in an unheated shed in a Nebraska winter?

Only if that battery's datasheet allows charging at the temperature in the shed. Many LiFePO4 packs block charging near freezing. The battery column on this page is energy capacity (daily kWh divided by 0.81). It is not a cold-weather charging approval. See the battery chemistry guide for the published temperature windows.
